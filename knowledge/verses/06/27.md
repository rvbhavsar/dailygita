# Chapter 6, Verse 27

Part of [Dhyana Yoga](/chapters/06.md).

## Sanskrit

प्रशान्तमनसं ह्येनं योगिनं सुखमुत्तमम्।

उपैति शान्तरजसं ब्रह्मभूतमकल्मषम्।।6.27।।

## Transliteration

praśhānta-manasaṁ hyenaṁ yoginaṁ sukham uttamam
upaiti śhānta-rajasaṁ brahma-bhūtam akalmaṣham

## Translations

- *Swami Adidevananda*: For supreme happiness comes to the Yogin whose mind is at peace, who is free from evil, from whom Rajas has departed, and who has become the Brahman.
- *Swami Gambirananda*: Supreme Bliss comes to this yogi alone whose mind has become perfectly tranquil, whose quality of rajas has been eliminated, who has become identified with Brahman, and is untainted.
- *Swami Sivananda*: Supreme Bliss indeed comes to this Yogi whose mind is made peaceful, whose passion is quelled, who has become Brahman, and who is free from sin.
- *Dr. S. Sankaranarayan*: Indeed, the Supreme Bliss comes to this highly tranquil-minded man of Yoga, whose passions remain stilled, who has become one with the Brahman, and who is free from sins.
- *Shri Purohit Swami*: Supreme bliss is the lot of the sage whose mind attains peace, whose passions subside, who is without sin, and who becomes one with the Absolute.

## Word meanings

praśhānta—peaceful; manasam—mind; hi—certainly; enam—this; yoginam—yogi; sukham uttamam—the highest bliss; upaiti—attains; śhānta-rajasam—whose passions are subdued; brahma-bhūtam—endowed with God-realization; akalmaṣham—without sin

## Themes

The deep peace and lasting joy that comes when the mind becomes truly tranquil and clear.

**Keywords:** supreme bliss, deep peace, tranquility, calm mind, inner joy, contentment, serenity, lasting happiness, freedom from turmoil, pure mind
