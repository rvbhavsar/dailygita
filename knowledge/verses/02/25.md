# Chapter 2, Verse 25

Part of [Sankhya Yoga](/chapters/02.md).

## Sanskrit

अव्यक्तोऽयमचिन्त्योऽयमविकार्योऽयमुच्यते।

तस्मादेवं विदित्वैनं नानुशोचितुमर्हसि।।2.25।।

## Transliteration

avyakto ’yam achintyo ’yam avikāryo ’yam uchyate
tasmādevaṁ viditvainaṁ nānuśhochitum arhasi

## Translations

- *Swami Adidevananda*: This (self) is said to be unmanifest, inconceivable, and unchanging. Therefore, knowing it thus, it does not befit you to grieve.
- *Swami Gambirananda*: It is said that This is unmanifest; This is inconceivable; This is unchangeable. Therefore, having known This thus, you ought not to grieve.
- *Swami Sivananda*: This Self is said to be unmanifested, unthinkable, and unchangeable. Therefore, knowing this to be so, you should not grieve.
- *Dr. S. Sankaranarayan*: This is declared to be non-evident, imponderable, and unchangeable. Therefore, understanding this as such, you should not lament.
- *Shri Purohit Swami*: It is named the Unmanifest, the Unthinkable, and the immutable. Therefore, knowing the Spirit as such, you have no cause to grieve.

## Word meanings

avyaktaḥ—unmanifested; ayam—this soul; achintyaḥ—inconceivable; ayam—this soul; avikāryaḥ—unchangeable; ayam—this soul; uchyate—is said; tasmāt—therefore; evam—thus; viditvā—having known; enam—this soul; na—not; anuśhochitum—to grieve; arhasi—befitting

## Themes

Easing grief by accepting that some deep truths are unseen, mysterious and unchanging.

**Keywords:** grief, loss, sorrow, acceptance, mystery, unknowable, unchangeable, letting go, peace, understanding, confusion, impermanence
