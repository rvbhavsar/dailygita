# Chapter 2, Verse 41

Part of [Sankhya Yoga](/chapters/02.md).

## Sanskrit

व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिरेकेह कुरुनन्दन।

बहुशाखा ह्यनन्ताश्च बुद्धयोऽव्यवसायिनाम्।।2.41।।

## Transliteration

vyavasāyātmikā buddhir ekeha kuru-nandana
bahu-śhākhā hyanantāśh cha buddhayo ’vyavasāyinām

## Translations

- *Swami Adidevananda*: In this (Karma Yoga), O Arjuna, the resolute mind is single-pointed; the minds of the irresolute are multi-branched and endless.
- *Swami Gambirananda*: O scion of the Kuru dynasty, in this there is a single, one-pointed conviction. The thoughts of the irresolute ones, however, have many branches indeed and are innumerable.
- *Swami Sivananda*: Here, O joy of the Kurus, there is only one single-pointed determination; many-branched and endless are the thoughts of the indecisive.
- *Dr. S. Sankaranarayan*: O source of joy to the Kurus! The knowledge in the form of determination is one; however, the knowledge (thoughts) of those who lack determination has many branches and has no end.
- *Shri Purohit Swami*: By its means, the straying intellect becomes steadied in contemplating one object only; whereas the minds of the irresolute stray into innumerable bypaths.

## Word meanings

vyavasāya-ātmikā—resolute; buddhiḥ—intellect; ekā—single; iha—on this path; kuru-nandana—descendent of the Kurus; bahu-śhākhāḥ—many-branched; hi—indeed; anantāḥ—endless; cha—also; buddhayaḥ—intellect; avyavasāyinām—of the irresolute

## Themes

Having one firm resolve brings clarity, while lack of resolve leaves you with endless scattered thoughts.

**Keywords:** determination, focused, scattered, overwhelmed, clarity, indecision, priorities, single, purpose, confusion, centered, resolve
