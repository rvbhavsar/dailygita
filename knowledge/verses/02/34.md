# Chapter 2, Verse 34

Part of [Sankhya Yoga](/chapters/02.md).

## Sanskrit

अकीर्तिं चापि भूतानि कथयिष्यन्ति तेऽव्ययाम्।

संभावितस्य चाकीर्तिर्मरणादतिरिच्यते।।2.34।।

## Transliteration

akīrtiṁ chāpi bhūtāni
kathayiṣhyanti te ’vyayām
sambhāvitasya chākīrtir
maraṇād atirichyate

## Translations

- *Swami Adidevananda*: Further, people will speak ill of you for all time, and for one accustomed to being honored, dishonor is worse than death.
- *Swami Gambirananda*: People also will speak of your unending infamy, and to an honored person, infamy is worse than death.
- *Swami Sivananda*: People will also recount your everlasting dishonor; and for one who has been honored, dishonor is worse than death.
- *Dr. S. Sankaranarayan*: The creatures will speak of your endless ill-fame; and for one who has been highly esteemed, ill-fame is worse than death.
- *Shri Purohit Swami*: Men will talk forever of thy disgrace; and to the noble, dishonor is worse than death.

## Word meanings

akīrtim—infamy; cha—and; api—also; bhūtāni—people; kathayiṣhyanti—will speak; te—of your; avyayām—everlasting; sambhāvitasya—of a respectable person; cha—and; akīrtiḥ—infamy; maraṇāt—than death; atirichyate—is greater
