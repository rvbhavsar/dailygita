# Chapter 2, Verse 15

Part of [Sankhya Yoga](/chapters/02.md).

## Sanskrit

यं हि न व्यथयन्त्येते पुरुषं पुरुषर्षभ।

समदुःखसुखं धीरं सोऽमृतत्वाय कल्पते।।2.15।।

## Transliteration

yaṁ hi na vyathayantyete puruṣhaṁ puruṣharṣhabha
sama-duḥkha-sukhaṁ dhīraṁ so ’mṛitatvāya kalpate

## Translations

- *Swami Adidevananda*: For, he whom these do not affect, O chief of men, and to whom pain and pleasure are equal - that steadfast man alone is worthy of immortality.
- *Swami Gambirananda*: O Arjuna, who is foremost among men, verily, the person whom these do not torment, the wise man to whom sorrow and happiness are the same—he is fit for immortality.
- *Swami Sivananda*: That firm man, whom surely these afflictions do not, O chief among men, to whom pleasure and pain are the same, is fit for attaining immortality.
- *Dr. S. Sankaranarayan*: O best among persons! That wise person becomes immortal, whom these situations do not trouble, and to whom pleasure and pain are equal.
- *Shri Purohit Swami*: The hero whose soul is unmoved by circumstances, who accepts pleasure and pain with equanimity, is only fit for immortality.

## Word meanings

yam—whom; hi—verily; na—not; vyathayanti—distressed; ete—these; puruṣham—person; puruṣha-ṛiṣhabha—the noblest amongst men, Arjun; sama—equipoised; duḥkha—distress; sukham—happiness; dhīram—steady; saḥ—that person; amṛitatvāya—for liberation; kalpate—becomes eligible

## Themes

Staying equally steady through happiness and sorrow without being thrown off balance.

**Keywords:** staying steady, equanimity, balanced mind, not reactive, calm under pressure, unshaken, even tempered, inner composure, resilience, not tormented, steady through highs and lows
